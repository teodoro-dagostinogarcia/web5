Handicapping

How association croquet handicaps work and how yours can change.

On this page
28 to 16 handicap range14 to 10 handicap range9 handicap and belowAbility TableSimple Test

Association croquet uses a handicap system so players of different standards can compete on roughly equal terms. Handicaps run from 28 for a novice to negative handicaps for the strongest players.

Handicaps change with time - as they say on the adverts, " handicaps may go up as well as down ". Our aim is to give you the correct handicap to have an equal chance of winning. No one benefits from an incorrect handicap.

The Club has a Croquet England National Handicapper, Ian Plummer, who can set official handicaps.

28 to 16 handicap range

Novices normally start at 28. As you improve, a handicap can be adjusted through observation and simple assessment.

14 to 10 handicap range

Ian will be involved in adjusting handicaps in this range.

9 handicap and below

Once you are established as a player, tournament and inter-club results provide the normal basis for automatic handicap adjustment.

Simple Test

To gauge your handicap in the 28 to 12 range, the following routine is suggested on a full-sized court with standard equipment. Place a ball in Corner 2, a ball level with hoop 1 on its adjacent yard-line, and two balls a metre apart on the boundary to the side of hoop four. Now, using bisque (free) turns, play with one of the balls by hoop 4 and count how many bisque turns you need to take to get the ball to the peg (all 12 hoops). Doubling the number of bisques taken indicates your approximate handicap.

Updated February 2018 to reflect that the Half Blue qualifying handicap being raised from 6 to 8.
